Sustainable investing under uncertainty: A dual-criterion probabilistic framework

Bongermino, G., Romagnoli, S. & Rossi, P. (2026)

Decisions in Economics and Finance

Published online 28 July 2026

Abstract

This paper develops a probabilistic approach to sustainable portfolio allocation when ESG scores provide imperfect information. Portfolio weights are treated as random variables that reflect both financial and sustainability preferences. A penalty for weak ESG signals shifts the allocation toward financially robust investments when sustainability information is less informative. The empirical analysis reports more diversified portfolios, reduced sensitivity to parameter choices, and more stable out-of-sample performance than the expected-utility ESG portfolios used for comparison.

ESG rating and ambiguity: an informative and distorted signal-based approach

Bongermino, G. & Romagnoli, S. (2026)

Decisions in Economics and Finance, 49, 275–316

First published online 13 January 2025

Abstract

This paper models ESG ratings by distinguishing the quality of firms' disclosed information from investors' subjective interpretation of that information. Differences in disclosure, confidence, and attitudes toward ambiguity can distort sustainability assessments. The proposed framework uses these distortions to study the robustness of ESG scores and identify sectors whose sustainability may be overestimated or underestimated, with implications for the assessment of policy interventions.

Modeling Commodity Futures with Realized Volatility

Work in progress

Abstract

In light of the recent crisis in the European energy system, we propose an analysis of futures contracts written on commodities representative of the key factors of production in the EU economy: Dutch TTF, Brent crude oil—for its ties to inflation and economic activity—and Carbon Allowances of the European Trading Scheme—for their role in the sustainable transition, as production costs for polluting firms.

We take innovative methods, recently introduced for equity indexes and successful in capturing their volatility, and apply them to commodity futures. They take as input high-frequency trade data, which is used to compute a measure of daily realized volatility that incorporates, by construction, information about intra-day transactions. We test the realized volatility for its power in explaining daily close-to-close returns volatility, to understand if the data-rich model, as happens in the equity market, brings enough benefits to justify its use. We find that, for the commodity futures under study, the answer is negative, based on a Value-at-Risk validation test.

We also blend this frontier method with classical univariate and multivariate financial time-series modeling. The realized volatility is included as an exogenous regressor inside multiple different univariate GARCH(p,q) model specifications. We find that the realized volatility consistently improves the quality of the fit and is a significant explanatory factor of the close-to-close volatility, but its predictive power is unsatisfactory. In the multivariate setting the interaction terms between the variances of all commodities are included and we are able to highlight the simultaneous transmission channels between them.
